THE BUSINESS PROBLEM

Leadership needed faster answers without losing analytical context.

Recurring reporting can show what happened during a single period while leaving decision makers to determine whether a change is unusual, where it is concentrated, and what is driving it.

1What is happening now?
2How does it compare with prior periods?
3Where and what are the main drivers?
4What should leadership notice first?

ANALYTICAL DESIGN

One measure layer, multiple levels of analysis.

1. KPI layer

Reusable measures provide recent-period totals, prior-period comparisons, year-over-year context, and YTD performance without duplicating business logic across visuals.

2. Diagnostic layer

Category, region, subregion, and time dimensions let users move from the executive summary into the specific drivers behind a change.

3. Data-quality layer

Preparation steps standardize values, validate relationships, isolate exceptions, and preserve clear business definitions before data reaches the reporting layer.

4. Interpretation layer

Dynamic narrative logic converts changing filter context into concise text so the report surfaces the most important movements rather than requiring readers to interpret every visual independently.

BUSINESS IMPACT

The value is not the visual—it is the reduction in analytical friction.

This design brings recurring KPI logic, comparison periods, drill-down paths, and narrative interpretation into one governed reporting experience. The result is faster review, more consistent definitions, easier identification of exceptions, and a clearer path from executive overview to detailed investigation.
